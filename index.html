How It Works

Drones in the air over the valley, every day of fire season

The valley stopped sending firefighters to new fires first in 2043. Fires are now spotted, assigned, and fought by machines that run without waiting for anyone to tell them to start.

Spotting the fire

Heat and smoke sensors sit on 46 towers along the ridges between Peachland and Lake Country. They also track lightning strikes. A new fire is usually spotted within 31 seconds of starting.

Sending the drones

Our software chooses which drones go, which lake they fill from, and how they surround the fire. No person approves this first; the system acts on its own. Fire Command staff can stop or redirect the drones after they launch, but they are not the ones who send them.

Putting it out

Each drone carries 400 litres of water, refilled at one of 22 stations along the lakes. Between six and forty drones work a single fire at once, taking turns to refill, until the fire is surrounded and out.

Why we built this

The Okanagan Mountain Park fire of 2003 destroyed 239 homes. The McDougall Creek fire of 2023 destroyed 189 more and forced 35,000 people to leave in a single night.

By the late 2030s, fires were starting faster than firefighters could drive to them. The drones solved that problem. They did not solve the other one: there is still only so much water in the lake.

Water

There is not enough water for every fire. Here is how we decide.

Okanagan Lake has been below its normal level since 2041. By law, the water we may use for firefighting is capped each year, and shared with drinking water and farms. Aeris does not set that cap. We decide how to spend what we are given.

Before each fire season, every home is given a protection tier. Your tier sets how much water is held for your address, and what the drones will do if fire reaches you. Tiers are reviewed once a year, based on how your house is built, how much brush is near it, how likely fire is to spread through your area, and whether your sensors are working.

A tier is a planning decision about a building. It is not a judgement about the people who live there. We publish how tiers are set so that you can see the standard we applied to your home, and what you can do about it.

Full protection
Tier 1

Structure Defence

12,000 L held per season

The drones will protect your house.

Drones will wet the ground around your property line, then keep water on the building itself until the fire passes or your water allowance runs out.

To stay in Tier 1, your home needs:

  • Brush and trees cleared 30 m around the house, checked in the last year
  • A fire-rated roof, and vents that block flying embers
  • All four roof sensors working and connected
  • Signed permission for drones to fly over your land and draw water from your pool
Protection when water allows
Tier 2

Structure Defence, Conditional

6,000 L held per season

The drones will protect your house, unless water runs short.

We will protect the building when there is enough water. At Stage 4 or higher, we may stop protecting your house partway through a fire and switch you to Tier 3 without warning you first. If that happens, it is recorded in your property file.

Homes are placed in Tier 2 when:

  • Something was still unfinished at the last inspection
  • Or the house is fine, but fire is more likely to spread through your area than most
  • Your water allowance is used up as the season goes on, and is not topped back up
No water on the building
Tier 3

Perimeter Defence

No water held for the building

The drones will not put water on your house.

Drones will try to slow the fire moving through your neighbourhood. They will not put water on your house. You will be told to evacuate before anyone else is, as soon as a fire is confirmed.

Homes are placed in Tier 3 when:

  • The brush inspection is overdue, was failed, or the inspector was turned away
  • Or the house sits somewhere our drones cannot work effectively
  • Or we do not have signed permission to fly over the property
  • You can ask for a free re-inspection once each season

Businesses, farms, schools, and apartment buildings are assessed under separate rules. Hospitals, water treatment, and power stations are protected at every water stage, outside the tier system.

What We Need From You

Four things keep your house protected

Our drones can only protect a house they can reach, measure, and plan around. These four things are part of the agreement, not suggestions.

01

Yearly brush inspection

An Aeris inspector walks your property and records how much burnable material is within 30 m of the house. Booking opens March 1. If you miss it or turn the inspector away, your home moves to Tier 3 at the next review.

02

Roof sensors

Four small units on your roof send us temperature, ember, and moisture readings. We install them free. If they stay offline more than 14 days, your home loses Tier 1 standing.

03

Drone access permission

A legal agreement letting our drones fly low over your property, and pump water out of your pool or pond during a fire.

04

How your house is built

A fire-rated roof, vents that block embers, and nothing that burns within 1.5 m of any wall. Houses built before 2038 can apply for help with the cost.

Assured Response

For $1,840 a year, you can buy an extra 9,000 litres of water that is held for your home no matter how low the lake gets. This water sits outside the yearly limit.

Assured Response is open to Tier 1 and Tier 2 homes. It does not change your tier, and it does not move you ahead of other customers who have also paid.

Questions Residents Ask

Straight answers about how we decide

If two homes are threatened at once and there is water for one, how is the choice made?

The software sends drones where they will stop the most fire overall, and it gives weight to how much water each home has left. If two homes are equal on both counts, the higher tier gets the water.

The decision takes under two seconds, and it is recorded. You can ask to see the record for any fire near your home.

Can a human stop the drones during a fire?

Yes. Fire Command staff can stop or redirect the drones at any time, and they do so about eleven times a season. What they cannot do is create water that the yearly cap does not allow. Sending water to one house means another house gets less.

Why is my whole neighbourhood Tier 3?

Older houses, smaller lots, and steep narrow roads make it harder to meet our requirements and harder for drones to work. Those neighbourhoods end up with more Tier 2 and Tier 3 homes.

Help is available: the Retrofit Assistance Program pays up to 60% of the cost of upgrading a house, for homes assessed below the regional average. The Province sets how many households the program can take each year. It was full in 2049 and full again in 2050.

What if my water runs out halfway through the season?

Your home moves to Tier 3 for the rest of the season. Drones will still work to slow fire in your area, and you will still be warned to leave early. Water allowances cannot be given to another household, and unused water does not carry over to next year.

Can I appeal my tier?

Yes. You have 14 days from when your tier is set. Appeals are heard by the Allocation Review Board, which meets weekly from April to June. The Board can order a new inspection if your last one got something factually wrong. It cannot change the rules themselves, which are set by law.

Board decisions are final. In 2050, 2,847 people appealed and 319 had their tier changed.

Who else sees my sensor data?

We use your sensor data to set your tier, and we share it with the Province for water planning. We do not give it to insurance companies. Insurance companies usually ask you for your tier themselves, before they will cover your home.
