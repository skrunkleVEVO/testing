The Network

A permanent aerial presence over the wildland interface

The valley has not relied on crewed initial attack since 2043. Detection, dispatch, and suppression are continuous, automated, and basin-wide.

Detection

Thermal and spectral sensors on 46 ridge masts between Peachland and Lake Country, cross-referenced with lightning telemetry. Median time from ignition to confirmed detection is 31 seconds.

Dispatch

The Basin Allocation Engine selects an aircraft group, a water source, and a suppression envelope without human initiation. Regional Fire Command may override a dispatch but does not authorise one.

Suppression

Each aircraft carries 400 litres drawn from one of 22 lake-fed charging pads. Groups of six to forty operate on a shared containment geometry and refill on rotation until the envelope closes.

Why the network exists

The Okanagan Mountain Park fire of 2003 destroyed 239 homes. The McDougall Creek fire of 2023 destroyed 189 and displaced 35,000 people in a single night. By the late 2030s the valley was recording interface ignitions faster than crewed resources could reach them. Autonomy closed that gap. It did not create more water.

Water Allocation

In a draw-limited season, suppression water is finite and assigned in advance

Okanagan Lake has operated below its historical licensed draw since 2041. Under the Basin Water Order, suppression withdrawals are capped seasonally and shared with municipal supply and agriculture. Aeris does not set the cap. We allocate what the cap permits.

Every residential property in the service area is assigned a protection tier before the season opens. The tier determines how much suppression water is reserved for that address and what the network will do when a fire approaches it. Tiers are recalculated annually from a property's structural assessment, its vegetation audit, its position in the modelled fire-spread network, and its sensor mesh status.

Tier assignment is a planning instrument, not a judgement about the people who live at an address. It is published so that residents can see the standard applied to their property and act on it.

Full envelope
Tier 1

Structure Defence

12,000 L reserved per season

The network will defend the building itself. On approach, aircraft establish a wetted perimeter at the property line and hold a structure-directed envelope until the front passes or the allowance is exhausted.

  • Verified defensible space to 30 m, audited within 12 months
  • Class A roofing and ember-resistant soffit and vent assemblies
  • Active sensor mesh with uninterrupted uplink
  • Signed overflight and drafting easement on file
Conditional
Tier 2

Structure Defence, Conditional

6,000 L reserved per season

Structure defence is provided when basin draw status permits it. At Stage 4 and above, Tier 2 properties may be reassigned to perimeter defence during an active event without advance notice. Reassignment is logged and viewable in your property record.

  • One or more compliance items outstanding at last audit
  • Or: structural assessment complete, modelled spread risk above basin median
  • Allowance is drawn down across the season and is not restored
Perimeter only
Tier 3

Perimeter Defence

No structure-directed allowance

The network will work to contain fire movement through the surrounding area. It will not direct water at the building. Tier 3 properties receive priority evacuation notification at the earliest confirmed detection, routed ahead of general alerting.

  • Vegetation audit overdue, failed, or access refused
  • Or: structure outside the serviceable envelope for the available aircraft group
  • Or: easement not on file
  • Reassessment available once per season at no charge

Commercial, agricultural, institutional, and multi-unit residential properties are assessed under a separate schedule. Critical infrastructure is protected outside the tier system at all draw stages.

What We Ask of Residents

Four obligations keep a property in structure defence

The network can only protect what it can reach, measure, and model. These requirements are conditions of the service agreement, not recommendations.

01

Annual vegetation audit

An Aeris assessor walks the property and records fuel loading within 30 m. Bookings open 1 March. A refused or missed audit moves the property to Tier 3 at the next recalculation.

02

Sensor mesh

Four roof-mounted units report temperature, ember flux, and moisture to the allocation engine. Units are installed at no cost. A mesh offline for more than 14 days suspends Tier 1 standing.

03

Overflight and drafting easement

A registered covenant permitting aircraft transit below 120 m over the parcel and, where a pond or pool is present, drafting from it during an active event.

04

Structural compliance

Class A roofing, ember-resistant vents, and no combustible material within 1.5 m of any wall. Properties built before 2038 may apply to the Retrofit Assistance Program.

Assured Response

Households that wish to hold structure defence independently of basin draw status may enrol in Assured Response, a supplementary allowance of 9,000 litres carried outside the seasonal cap. Enrolment is $1,840 per year and is available to Tier 1 and Tier 2 properties. Assured Response does not change tier assignment and does not alter dispatch priority between enrolled properties.

Questions Residents Ask

Straight answers about how the network decides

If two homes are threatened at once and there is water for one, how is the choice made?

The allocation engine resolves competing demands by modelled containment value: the aircraft group is committed where suppression is expected to prevent the greatest total spread, weighted by tier allowance remaining at each address. Where containment value is equal, the engine commits to the property with the higher tier.

The decision is made in under two seconds and is recorded. Residents may request the decision log for any event affecting their property.

Can a person override a dispatch decision during a fire?

Regional Fire Command can halt or redirect an aircraft group at any time and does so, on average, eleven times per season. Command cannot create an allocation that the Basin Water Order does not permit. An override that commits water to one property reduces what is available to the rest of the envelope.

My neighbourhood is mostly Tier 3. Why?

Tier distribution is not uniform across the valley. Areas with older housing stock, smaller lots, or steep access roads tend toward Tier 2 and Tier 3 because retrofit compliance is harder to achieve and serviceable envelopes are narrower. Aeris publishes tier distribution by neighbourhood in the Annual Service Report.

The Retrofit Assistance Program covers up to 60% of compliance costs for properties assessed below the regional median. Program capacity is set by the Province and was fully subscribed in 2049 and 2050.

What happens if my allowance runs out mid-season?

A property with an exhausted allowance is placed on perimeter defence for the remainder of the season. The network continues to contain fire movement around the address and evacuation notification continues at priority. Allowances are not transferable between properties and do not carry forward.

Can I appeal my tier?

Yes. Appeals are filed within 14 days of assignment and are heard by the Allocation Review Board, which sits weekly from April to June. The Board may order a reassessment where an audit is shown to contain a factual error. It does not review the allocation model itself, which is set under the Basin Water Order.

Board decisions are final. In 2050, 2,847 appeals were filed and 319 resulted in a tier change.

Is my sensor data used for anything other than fire response?

Mesh data informs tier recalculation and is shared with the Province for basin modelling. It is not shared with insurers. Insurers may, and generally do, ask residents to disclose their tier directly as a condition of coverage.
